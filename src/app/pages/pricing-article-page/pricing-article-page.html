Website Design Prices in Cape Town (2026): What You Actually Pay

Ask five Cape Town web designers what a website costs and you will get five numbers spanning two orders of magnitude — from R500 to R150,000. All five can be "correct". This guide explains what sits behind each number, where the traps are, and what our own packages cost, so you can compare quotes knowing what you are actually buying.

The honest market overview

The Cape Town website market breaks into four broad price bands, and each one is a different product wearing the same name:

  • Template mills — R500 to R2,500 once-off. Your logo and text dropped into a pre-made theme, often in a single afternoon.
  • Freelancers — R2,500 to R10,000. Wide quality range: some excellent value, some template work at freelance prices.
  • Agencies — R25,000 to R50,000+ to start. Professional output with account managers, process and overheads built into the price.
  • Subscription builders — R499 to R799 per month. "No upfront cost" website rental. The catch gets its own section below.

What you get — and don't — at each level

Templates (R500–R2,500) are honest products if you need any web presence at all, today, for almost nothing. What you don't get: design decisions made for your business, pages Google can rank, or anything that differentiates you from the other businesses using the same theme. If a customer would ever compare you against a competitor, this tier usually costs more in lost credibility than it saves.

Freelancers (R2,500–R10,000) can be the best value in the market — a skilled independent without agency overheads. The risk is not skill, it's continuity: the same person doing your build is doing support, and freelancers change careers, emigrate and disappear. Ask what happens to hosting, updates and emergencies in year two.

Agencies (R25,000+) deliver reliably professional work with process around it. For corporates that need contracts, compliance and a team on call, the premium is justified. For a small business wanting a site that brings in customers, a large share of the invoice is paying for the machinery, not the website.

Subscription builders (R499–R799/month) hand you a finished-looking site quickly with nothing upfront — genuinely attractive if cash flow is tight. Read the next section before signing anything.

The subscription trap, with real numbers

R499/month sounds like a fraction of an R8,500 once-off build. Run the numbers over the period you will actually keep the website and the picture inverts — and the bigger problem is not the total, it's the last row of this table:

Once-off build vs R499/month subscription over time
Once-off (Bronze, R8,500) Subscription (R499/month)
Cost after 12 months R8,500 R5,988
Cost after 24 months R8,500 R11,976
Cost after 48 months R8,500 R23,952
If you stop paying Site stays yours, stays online Site is switched off
Own the domain, code & content Yes — all of it Usually the domain at best

Somewhere between month 14 and month 18 the subscription passes the once-off price — and keeps going, forever, without you ever owning anything. The website becomes rent. Every rand you have paid evaporates the day you cancel, which also makes it painful to ever switch providers. That lock-in, not the monthly fee, is the real product.

How to compare the quotes you're holding

Whoever you end up hiring — us or anyone else — five questions will expose the real differences between quotes that all say "website" on them:

  • Who owns everything when it's done? Domain in whose name, code and content delivered how. If the answer is vague, the price is not the real price.
  • What exactly is included in the number? Ask specifically about copywriting, images, number of revisions, and mobile — the classic "that's extra" items.
  • What happens after launch? Hosting cost, support arrangements, and what a small change (a price update, a new photo) will cost you in month six.
  • Can Google actually read it? Ask whether the site ships with real page titles, descriptions and a sitemap. Many cheap builds are invisible to search engines — which quietly cancels the reason you bought a website.
  • Can you speak to a past client? A builder proud of their work will hand you a name within the hour.

Any quote that survives those five questions is worth considering, at any price band. Most don't survive the first one.
